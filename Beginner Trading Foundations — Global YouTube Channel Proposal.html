01

Executive summary

Trading education has plenty of content. What beginners lack is a clear starting point and an order to learn in.

Problem

A flood of content, no clear starting point

Beginner requests for learning paths come up repeatedly on Reddit, and the replies are mostly reading lists and contradictory advice.

More fundamentally, many beginners place trades before they understand what they are trading, how leverage magnifies losses, or how costs add up.

Solution

28 numbered episodes, each resolving one point of confusion

01Differentiation

Sequence

Each episode resolves one confusion and builds on the one before.

02Differentiation

No sales pitch

No platform, instrument or parameter recommendations, and no market calls.

03Differentiation

Depth

Every formula comes with one full worked example, and every episode names and corrects one common beginner misconception.

Expected valueA credible educational content asset that anchors the global channel from launch. A transparent, neutral stance reduces the brand-trust risk that comes with advertorial-style content.

03

Positioning & boundaries

This series does not teach how to make money. It covers what to learn first, what to learn next and why, and makes the maths of each step clear.

In scope
  • The manual trading path, from zero to the first demo trade
  • Real instrument names as examples — main: EUR/USD and US500; comparison: XAU/USD and WTI crude
  • ZERO Markets' real specifications (contract size, minimum volume, value per point) where possible, so they match screen recordings
  • Platform- or jurisdiction-dependent rules (stop-out level, triple-swap day, leverage caps, negative balance protection) explained as mechanisms only
Out of scope
  • Algorithmic trading, specific strategies and technical-analysis patterns
  • Broker, platform or instrument recommendations
  • Specific parameters: risk per trade, leverage ratio or deposit amount
  • Market calls, implied returns, or "do this to make money" statements

06

Standard episode structure

Every episode runs 2 to 3.5 minutes on the same timeline. Pick a length and click a segment.

Every body unit follows four steps

1

Definition

One sentence on what it is; any analogy comes after the definition.

2

Worked example

Run the full calculation with numbers, each step appearing on screen in turn.

3

Misconception

Name one common beginner misconception and correct it.

4

Quick quiz

One multiple-choice question per episode, after the most important unit — via YouTube's quiz feature where available.

What it looks like on screen

Two units from the plan, rebuilt as interactive previews: the #12 mid-video question and the #22 worked example. Numbers are illustrative, exactly as they'll be labelled in the videos.

#12 BID, ASK AND SPREADMID-VIDEO QUESTION
ILLUSTRATIVE
0.1 lot EUR/USD, $1 per pip, spread 1.2 pips. What does your P/L show right after opening?
You buy at the ask but the position is valued at the bid, so it opens 1.2 pips down: 1.2 × $1 = about −$1.20. A platform spread showing "12" is in points — 1.2 pips (see #7). Production standards

Dark background, a single accent colour, one consistent font for data labels. Calculations appear step by step, each step held long enough to follow.

How platform rules are presented

The mechanism itselfHow margin is calculated, how a stop order fills — explained directly.SOURCE · regulators' investor-education pages, IOSCO reports
Specific parametersStop-out level, leverage ratio, fees — illustrative numbers only, with "platform rules apply".SOURCE · no single platform cited
Jurisdiction-dependent protectionsNegative balance protection, leverage caps — "depends on your jurisdiction", directing viewers to their local regulator.SOURCE · each jurisdiction's regulator website

Short-form clip standards

  • Mark 1–2 clip-ready units per episode at script stage, favouring hooks, misconception corrections and analogies.
  • Reels and TikTok are always vertical 9:16 — re-lay out calculations and tables rather than cropping; keep text clear of the right-side buttons and bottom caption area.
  • Every clip carries the risk warning and keeps its "Illustrative" / "For illustration only" labels.
  • Don't cut anything that could read as trading instructions out of context, such as a standalone sizing formula or order setup.
  • Clips end by pointing to the full video and the series playlist.
